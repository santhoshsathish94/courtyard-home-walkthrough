🔧 Services: water · drainage · biogas · rain · electrical

How it works and why Water (blue cold · red hot · teal pump line)
  • One underground sump + rainwater tank (~6,000 L, SE) takes municipal water and filtered roof rain. One pump with an auto level controller fills the overhead tank, so it never runs dry or overflows.
  • Overhead tank 1,500 L sits right over the two first-floor baths, raised to 5′-6″ so the showers get ~8′ of head without a pressure pump.
  • Three short downtakes: the bath core goes straight down the shared wall; the west line goes down the bedroom/stair wall to the balcony tap and the common bath; the kitchen line drops once in the SE corner of the south wall: the kitchen sink, RO, dishwasher and fridge dispenser on the inside, the foot-wash tap at the porch, and the utility sink and washer of the wash court on the outside, back to back.
  • A solar water heater beside the tank feeds both upstairs baths with a ~5′ hot run. The common bath gets a small 3 L geyser instead of a 40′ hot pipe that would waste water while you wait.
Drainage + biogas (brown soil · grey waste · yellow gas)
  • Toilet waste goes to a biogas digester (~2 m³, NW, buried), and kitchen scraps go into its feed chute. A gas pipe along the plinth runs to the hob on the east wall, dropping under the kitchen-door landing so nothing crosses the doorway: about 0.3–0.5 m³ a day, or 45–60 min on one burner (a supplement to LPG). The digester's overflow goes to a soak pit under the driveway (no street sewer).
  • Bath, kitchen, foot-wash and washer water stays out of the digester (soap stops the gas). It runs through a grease trap, then a sand filter, into a 700 L garden-reuse tank with a hand pump for the plants; its overflow goes to the same soak pit.
  • The first-floor stacks run outside the north wall on a solid stretch, so there are no leaks indoors, with one vent above the terrace.
  • The basement is tanked (waterproofed walls and raft). A small sump in its plant room has an auto pump that lifts any seepage to the grey line.
  • Check with your engineer: the 3′ side strip is tight next to the footing.
Rainwater (green)
  • The terrace slopes east to two outlets (NE + SE corners). One collector takes the rain through a first-flush + filter into the SE tank. The balcony pipe joins along the south boundary, and tank overflow goes to a recharge pit.
  • About 800 sq ft of roof × ~1 m of rain a year ≈ 60,000 L a year captured.
Electrical (orange)
  • Meter + main switch at the gate. The main DB is inside by the main door, and the first-floor DB is directly above it (one riser). Each room gets one switchboard by its door.
  • Circuits: lights + fans per floor; 6 A sockets per floor; 16 A for the kitchen, washer, pump, geyser and a carport EV/bike charger; one AC point per bedroom; outdoor lights on a photo-timer.
  • ~2 kWp solar on the existing pergola (~8 units a day), net-metered. The inverter is in the stair room, close to the panels.
  • LED lights, BLDC fans (~30 W instead of 75 W), 2-way switches on the stair, a motion sensor at the porch, and the skylight means no daytime lights in the hall.
  • Passive cooling does most of the work: the jaali stair and the courtyard lantern act as chimneys, and ventilators over doors and windows keep a cross-draught going, so fans and the bedroom ACs run far less.
  • Basement: an ERV (heat-recovery fresh-air unit, ~60 W, runs all the time) draws fresh air through a louvre hidden in the east plinth band, which is 2′ above the garden, and exhausts through a second louvre 5′ away. A dehumidifier keeps it dry. Lined, silenced ducts feed slot diffusers in the theatre ceiling.
