About this walkthrough

Concept visualisation, not a construction drawing. It shows design intent only. The structure (column and beam sizes, foundations), drainage sizing, setbacks, plot geometry and approvals all need a site survey and professional design by a licensed architect and structural engineer.

Plot: what was supplied and what is drawn

Owner's outline: the road (west) side is straight, 33 ft, and square to the north and south sides. South 46 ft, north 52 ft, so only the north-east corner reaches further east. The east side follows: √(33² + 6²) = 33 ft 6½ in.

Area = 33 × (46 + 52) / 2 = 1,617 sq ft, within 0.2 % of the stated 1,620 sq ft. An earlier note gave the north side as 51 ft and the east side as 33 ft, which cannot enclose 1,620 sq ft (maximum 1,596). The 52 ft north side reconciles the area. Corner angles, bearing and levels are not surveyed. Please confirm with the survey or FMB sketch.

Design intent

Structure16 columns on a 4 × 4 grid with 4.60 / 3.45 / 4.60 m bays from west to east. The north row and the outer lines stand in the boundary walls; the south row stands free along the edge of the south balcony. The four centre columns frame the court; the two at its open corners are round, exposed columns. The north-west bay and the west stair strip are cantilevers off column line A. No column moved for the balcony: only the rooms' south wall moved in.
GroundStacked use: parking below, the home above and a garden on the roof. The stilts are 2.85 m floor to floor (2.4 m clear under the beams). From the road: a slatted sliding car gate (south) to the car bay, and a separate 4 ft front gate (north) with a foot-wash tap. The front gate opens onto a covered entry walk under bedroom 3 that leads straight to the main stair: a straight 4 ft stair of 15 risers in flights of 5 + 10 with a mid landing, rising east along the north wall. The ground also has two-wheeler parking and the courtyard rain garden under the open sky. A terracotta-jaali wall screens the entry and the stair from the north neighbour.
First floorNine squares around a 3.2 × 2.6 m nadumuttam open to the sky, then a 5 ft covered balcony along the whole south side.
Centre: the main stair arrives on a covered landing in front of the 5 ft main door on column line C, which faces west and opens outward. A north walkway runs along the court's north side and a south walkway (the library walk) along its south side; it opens onto the balcony through a sliding door.
East (the open family half): the living / hall (NE), where you see the closed oak pooja on the east wall as you come through the main door (idols face west); the family space (E) with an oonjal facing the court; and the kitchen (SE): a breakfast bar with stools, the stove under a high exhaust jaali, and the fridge and sink along the south wall, with a sliding door to the balcony.
West: bedroom 3 (NW, with the extra width of the north-west bay) and the master bedroom (W). The common bath sits three steps up, over the main stair's first flight (its raised floor keeps 2.2 m headroom on the stair below), beside a short access with the doors to bedroom 3 and the bath. The master suite opens off the west end of the south walkway: a dressing, then the bedroom through an arch, with the ensuite in the south-west. Every door is at least 4 ft wide.
The rooms stop 5 ft short of the south edge: 33 ft of plot = two 6-inch compound walls + about 27 ft of rooms + the 5 ft balcony, so the south row holds only the ensuite, dressing and walkway, and bedroom 2 gave way to the balcony.
The terrace stair rises north from the balcony's west end in the open strip west of the bedrooms: 17 risers in flights of 9 + 8, 0.98 m clear (the strip is 1.03 m because the column line stays put). A full-height terracotta jaali on its road side and the stair itself shade the west walls. The route goes north (main stair), east (hall, family space, kitchen), south (balcony) and west (stair) to the terrace.
TerraceTerracotta-paved garden with planters, a PV pergola, a wash and drying area and a 1,000 L tank on a steel stand. The west stair arrives open to the sky on the north-west bay roof, so there is no stair head.
Passive coolingA low jaali inlet in the master bedroom's west wall (shaded by the west stair and its jaali) and the shaded stilts draw cooler air in. It crosses the court and the open family half. Warm air rises out through the open court and high east jaalis over the kitchen and the pooja. A jaali wall along the main stair lets air through the north side. The covered balcony shades every south wall; deep walls and rain hoods shade the other openings.
RainThe terrace falls to the court. Nine scuppers drop rain into the permeable court garden. Overflow runs east through a stone channel and silt trap to a recharge pit in the north-east garden, the extended corner. The first floor roofs the front strip, so nothing drains to the road.
MaterialsModern and natural: smooth white lime plaster, board-formed and fair-faced concrete, honed light stone floors through the open half and warm terrazzo in the bedrooms, terracotta jaalis and terrace pavers, flat stone coping, frameless glass guards, a floating oak stair on a steel spine, light oak joinery and timber-slat gates, and slim dark-aluminium windows and sliders.

What is checked automatically

Every published version passes a design validator. It checks that:

  • the supplied plot sides are exact, the east side is derived and the area is within 0.5 % of 1,620 sq ft
  • the 16 columns sit within the walls (except the two exposed court columns and the balcony row), and every beam span stays within span/depth ≤ 11
  • no beam crosses the court or stairwell
  • stair risers (≤ 190 mm), treads (≥ 250 mm), widths (4 ft main stair, ≥ 0.9 m terrace stair) and headroom (≥ 2.2 m, including under the raised bath) meet common rules
  • room sizes, 4 ft doors (5 ft main door), door swings and step-free circulation work
  • airflow paths pass only through real openings
  • drains fall to the recharge pit without reaching the road
  • the guards meet child-safety rules

It also runs browser tests on desktop and mobile. These are design-consistency checks, not code compliance or engineering approval.
