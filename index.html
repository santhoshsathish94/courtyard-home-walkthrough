About this walkthrough

Concept visualisation, not a construction drawing. It shows design intent only. The structure (column and beam sizes, foundations), drainage sizing, setbacks, plot geometry and approvals all need a site survey and professional design by a licensed architect and structural engineer.

Plot: what was supplied and what is drawn

Owner's outline: the road (west) side is straight, 33 ft, and square to the north and south sides. South 46 ft, north 52 ft, so only the north-east corner reaches further east. The east side follows: √(33² + 6²) = 33 ft 6½ in.

Area = 33 × (46 + 52) / 2 = 1,617 sq ft, within 0.2 % of the stated 1,620 sq ft. An earlier note gave the north side as 51 ft and the east side as 33 ft, which cannot enclose 1,620 sq ft (maximum 1,596). The 52 ft north side reconciles the area. Corner angles, bearing and levels are not surveyed. Please confirm with the survey or FMB sketch.

Design intent

Structure16 columns on a 4 × 4 grid. The outer rows stand in the north, south and east boundary walls, so the plan is free of internal columns. The four centre columns frame the court; the one at its open corner is a round, exposed column.
GroundAn open stilt garden. From the road: a slatted sliding car gate (south) to the car bay, and a separate front gate (north) with a foot-wash tap. The front gate leads straight along the entrance path to the foot of the stair, with a thinnai bench beside it. The ground also has two-wheeler parking and the courtyard rain garden under the open sky. A terracotta-jaali wall screens the stair from the north neighbour.
First floorNine squares around a 4.0 × 2.6 m nadumuttam open to the sky. One straight stair runs along the north wall, rising west to east, to an entrance foyer. The main door opens outward into the foyer and leads into the hall.
The north and east are open around the court: the hall with an oonjal facing the court, the living room with a closed pooja cabinet in the NE corner (idols face west), and the open kitchen. The kitchen is a U: a breakfast-counter leg with stools (no separate dining table), a stove leg under a high exhaust jaali, and a sink leg with the fridge at its mouth.
Four squares on the west and south hold three bedrooms: the master suite (west bath and dressing plus the SW bedroom, opening onto a full-width west balcony with a swing), bedroom 2 (south) and bedroom 3 (SE). Their walls and windows form the court's other two sides.
The NW square has the common bath and a utility lobby with a shared wash basin. The terrace stair starts from a pocket off this lobby, stacked above the main stair. No corridors.
TerraceTerracotta-paved garden with planters, a PV pergola, a wash and drying area, and the stair head carrying a 1,000 L tank.
Passive coolingLow jaali inlets in the south-west (master suite) and the shaded stilts draw cooler air in. It crosses the court and the open half of the house. Warm air rises out through the open court, the stair-head jaali and high east jaalis over the kitchen and the pooja. Deep walls and rain hoods shade the openings.
RainThe terrace falls to the court. Nine scuppers drop rain into the permeable court garden. Overflow runs east through a stone channel and silt trap to a recharge pit in the north-east garden, the extended corner. The balcony and the first floor roof the front strip, so nothing drains to the road.
MaterialsModern and natural: smooth white lime plaster, board-formed and fair-faced concrete, honed light stone floors through the open half and warm terrazzo in the bedrooms, terracotta jaalis and terrace pavers, flat stone coping, frameless glass guards, a floating oak stair on a steel spine, light oak joinery and timber-slat gates, and slim dark-aluminium windows and sliders.

What is checked automatically

Every published version passes a design validator. It checks that:

  • the supplied plot sides are exact, the east side is derived and the area is within 0.5 % of 1,620 sq ft
  • the 16 columns sit within the walls (except the one exposed column), and every beam spans ≤ 4.2 m
  • no beam crosses the court or stairwell
  • stair risers, treads and headroom (≥ 2.2 m) meet common rules
  • room sizes, door swings and step-free circulation work
  • airflow paths pass only through real openings
  • drains fall to the recharge pit without reaching the road
  • the guards meet child-safety rules

It also runs browser tests on desktop and mobile. These are design-consistency checks, not code compliance or engineering approval.
